4. Model architecture

The model is a standard encoder–decoder transformer of about 10 M parameters (Table X). The encoder reads the pulse sequence, the decoder writes the record, and six auxiliary heads on the encoder output provide the counts and per-pulse targets described in Section 3.

Input. Each pulse enters as the sum of six embeddings: a learned embedding of its quantised PRI (4096 linear bins over 1–1009 µs, twice the maximum PRI so that merged intervals still fall inside the grid), a linear projection of the continuous PRI, and learned embeddings of three integer channels computed from the signal — run length within the current level, recurrence (visits since the same level was last seen) and visit index counted from the end of the window. The sixth channel, time of arrival, is not embedded; it drives the positional encoding.

Positional encoding. The encoder uses rotary position embeddings (RoPE; Su et al., 2021), which rotate each query and key by an angle proportional to a position, so that the attention score between two pulses depends on the distance between them. We use two positions at once, in the axial form of Heo et al. (2024): half of the rotary dimension pairs in each head take their angle from the pulse's time of arrival, in units of about one pulse interval so that the time and index axes have comparable scales, and the other half from the pulse index. The reasoning is as follows. We assume that pulses can be dropped, so the pulse index is not a reliable distance: every index after a dropped pulse is shifted by one. Time of arrival is unaffected by dropout, and it is the coordinate in which periodicity lives — two pulses one cycle apart are one cycle apart in time whatever was lost in between. We also assume that dwell times are counted in pulses, so the model needs the number of pulses between two points as well, which only the index gives. With both, attention can compare elapsed time with pulse count, and a time gap larger than the index gap warrants is itself the signature of a dropped pulse. The 50/50 split was a design choice and was not tuned. Rotations are computed in fp32 even under mixed precision, since the angles grow large over a long window.

Encoder. Four pre-norm layers, eight heads, feed-forward width 1024, GELU, dropout 0.1. Queries and keys are layer-normalised before the rotation (QK normalisation; Henry et al., 2020). Without it the attention logits grew with the weight norms during training, the softmax collapsed towards one-hot and the gradient through attention died slowly, independently of the learning rate.

Decoder. Four standard pre-norm layers with causal self-attention and cross-attention to the encoder output. Its input is the token embedding plus a sinusoidal absolute position plus a learned segment position: the number of tokens since the last structural token (LEVELS, S, SEEN). This gives the decoder its place within the current block directly, so that writing the j-th state is a lookup of the j-th visit from the end rather than a count from a block start that depends on how many levels were listed. The output layer scores the vocabulary, masked at inference by the grammar and by the count and period heads.
